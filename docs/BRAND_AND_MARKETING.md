# Brand & Marketing Profile — "Callsheet"

_A research-grounded brand, color, and positioning strategy for the photographer
**bench + job-queue** marketplace. Compiled 2026-07-17. Every non-obvious market
claim is cited; figures flagged **[secondary]** recur across marketing/SEO
sources and should be traced to a primary origin (NAR, Redfin/VHT, etc.) before
use in an investor-facing document._

---

## 1. Executive summary

The market splits cleanly into two visual and strategic camps:

- **Light, blue/green, transactional marketplaces** — Snappr, Thumbtack, Fiverr,
  Upwork, Splento. They read as businesslike and legible for booking/dashboard
  flows. Green in particular is heavily "owned" by commodity gig platforms
  (Fiverr `#1DBF73`, Upwork `~#14A800`). [Sources: BrandColorCode — fiverr,
  upwork]
- **Dark, neutral, gallery-first creative tools** — Behance and the broader
  creative-portfolio culture, where the imagery _is_ the product and the UI gets
  out of the way. [Source: Behance dark-theme galleries]

**Our opportunity is to own the seam between them.** The company/booking side
wants the trust of the transactional camp; the photographer/showcase side wants
the gallery-first reverence of the creative camp. A **dark "gallery-wall" base +
off-white + a confident non-green accent** lets us look premium and creative
(not commodity) while a secondary trust-blue anchors the business side.

The single most defensible thing about the product is its answer to a
**documented structural failure**: freelance-photographer reliability and
scheduling break down at volume, and pure "Uber for photography" economics are
fragile (see Meero, §3). A **trained bench + atomic job queue** is the direct,
citable answer — and it should be the spine of the brand, not just a feature.

## 2. The market

- **Category size:** the commercial real-estate photography market is estimated
  at **~$3B (2025) growing to ~$5B by 2032 (~7% CAGR)** — other reports project
  ~10%, so treat as directional. [Source: Worldwide Market Reports via openPR]
- **Photography is now table stakes for listings:** US listings with pro photos
  rose from ~60% (2019) to **~85% (2023)**, with demand up ~25% YoY in 2023.
  **[secondary]** [Source: PhotoUp stats roundup]
- **The ROI pitch to companies/agents is strong:** professional photos are
  associated with homes selling **up to 47% more per sq ft**, **~32% faster**,
  and drawing **~61% more online views**. **[secondary — trace to primary before
  quoting]** [Sources: Luxury Presence; corroborated across pricing guides]
- **Typical 2026 rates** (useful for realistic marketplace pricing): ~$300–$600
  standard single-family (20–40 edited photos); $200–$400 small; $500–$1,200
  large/luxury with twilight; drone $150–$800. [Sources: Luxury Presence;
  RubyHome pricing guides]
- **Owner economics & the churn tell:** real-estate photography businesses can
  reach **$143K–$3M EBITDA**, and photographer **onboarding beyond ~14 days
  raises churn risk** — i.e. the training treadmill is a measurable drag on the
  business. [Source: FinancialModelsLab]

## 3. The core problem (our wedge)

The pain the product targets is well documented, not anecdotal:

- **Reliability is a structural failure, not a bad-luck event.** "A structural
  failure is built into the freelance photography model" — quality and
  consistency degrade with an individual shooter's energy, schedule, and
  burnout. [Source: Imagen]
- **Scheduling is fragile by design.** "A freelancer with a scheduling conflict
  means your listing waits, while a system with multiple certified technicians
  in your market means the shoot date holds." That sentence _is_ our value prop.
  [Source: Furoore]
- **Admin overload causes creative fatigue.** "61% of professional photographers
  cited administrative overload as a top reason for creative fatigue" — remove
  the coordination back-and-forth and you win loyalty. [Source: Furoore]
- **The cautionary tale:** Meero raised €205M+ as "Uber for photography," scaled
  100→750 staff, then abandoned the freelancer marketplace for AI SaaS and
  rebranded to Diffusely. Pure on-demand-freelancer economics got squeezed by
  commoditization and AI. [Source: French Tech Journal]

**Strategic implication:** do not position as "book any photographer, cheap."
Position as **reliability and consistency through a trained, rated bench** — the
opposite of the Meero model. The queue is how the bench scales; the bench is why
quality holds.

## 5. What photographers respond to (design & trust)

- **Dark "gallery-wall" backgrounds make imagery pop** — a museum-like framing
  where "each project emerges from darkness like artwork on gallery walls."
  [Sources: Lovable; Toptal Dark UI]
- **Dark mode is an expectation for creative/tech-savvy audiences**, not just a
  trend. [Source: Lovable]
- **The interface must be quieter than the work** — "make site colors quieter
  than your projects so thumbnails shine rather than fight for attention."
  [Source: DesignYourWay]
- **Portfolio-first exposure drives sign-up.** Creatives gravitate to platforms
  that showcase their work (Behance's whole draw; Contra's portfolio-first,
  zero-commission model). [Source: Lemon.io]
- **Reputation, reviews, and rigorous vetting build trust** (Trustpilot signals,
  verified payment, "top 3%"-style exclusivity). [Source: Lemon.io]
- **Fair economics matter** — low/no freelancer commission and transparent fees
  are repeatedly cited as sign-up drivers. [Source: Lemon.io]
- **Caveat:** dark UI can hurt readability on text-heavy/transactional screens —
  reinforcing a **split treatment**: gallery-dark for showcase, cleaner/legible
  for dashboards and forms. [Source: DigitalRoots]

**How the prototype already reflects this:** near-black canvas, imagery-led
cards, restrained chrome, portable ratings (`onTimeRate`, review counts,
portfolio galleries), transparent rates on every profile, and "trained/onboarding"
bench states as visible trust signals.

## 6. Brand name

### Recommendation: **Callsheet**

A **call sheet** is the document a production sends out that says _who_ is
shooting _what_, _where_, and _when_. That is precisely what this platform
digitizes — jobs, the bench, call times, coverage. The name is:

- **A tangible metaphor**, like the category's memorable names (Thumbtack =
  pin a job to a board; BoxBrownie = a camera). Coined/metaphor names dominate
  this space because descriptive names ("RealEstatePhotoPros") are hard to
  trademark and rank. [Naming pattern inferred from competitor set; Snappr/Soona/
  Aryeo/Meero are all coined.]
- **Authentic to the craft** — it signals "we speak your language," which the
  research ties directly to creative trust.
- **On-strategy** — it evokes coordination and reliability (the bench), not
  commodity gigs.

### Shortlist of alternates
- **Focal** — craft-rooted (focal length / focal point), friendly, premium;
  downside: "Focal" is a real word used by other brands (e.g. audio), harder to
  own outright.
- **Setly** — coined, warm, "on set" + familiar SaaS `-ly`; very likely
  ownable; downside: `-ly` reads slightly generic.
- **Onset** — "on set" + readiness; downside: strong "onset of \[illness]"
  connotation.
- **Photographylink** (current) — descriptive and clear, but weak to trademark
  and undifferentiated per the naming research.

> **Clearance required:** all names above need a formal USPTO + domain check
> before commitment. Note a possible collision with film-production tools that
> use "call sheet" (e.g. SetHero, Croogloo) — different category, but verify. A
> `.co` or a modified `.com` is a realistic fallback given `.com` scarcity in
> this space. The name is centralized in `lib/brand.ts`, so switching is a
> one-line change.

## 7. Visual system

### Direction: **dark gallery base + off-white + dual non-green accent**

This is the source-supported palette structure — "a signature hue, a
complementary partner, a tint/shade, a light neutral, and a dark anchor"
[Source: color-analysis.app] — tuned for photography, where **black/white/neutral
is the default sophistication signal** [Source: 99designs] and neutral
backgrounds exist to **let the subject shine** [Source: Innovature].

### Palette (as implemented in `tailwind.config.ts` / `lib/brand.ts`)

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `ink-950` | `#0a0a0b` | Dark anchor — the "gallery wall" canvas |
| `ink-900 / 800 / 700` | `#101012` → `#1f1f23` | Surfaces, cards, borders |
| `bone` | `#f4f1ea` | Warm off-white — primary text/light neutral |
| **`amber.brand`** | **`#e8a94b`** | **Signature accent — golden-hour warmth; the "claim / photographer" energy** |
| `amber.soft` | `#f0c078` | Accent tint for hover/emphasis |
| **slate blue** | **`#7c9cf5`** | **Complementary trust accent — the "company / booking" side** |

**Rationale for the dual accent:** the research shows the category divides into
warm/energetic (orange/amber → energy, "claim the job") and cool/trustworthy
(blue → professionalism, real-estate fit). Rather than pick one, we assign them
by side of the marketplace: **amber leads the photographer/claim experience;
blue anchors the company/trust experience.** Both are deliberately **non-green**
to separate from Fiverr/Upwork commodity signaling. [Sources: palmedesign;
99designs; BrandColorCode]

### Typography
- **Display:** `Fraunces` — an editorial serif that reads premium/creative, not
  SaaS-generic.
- **Body/UI:** `Inter` — highly legible for dashboards and forms (honoring the
  "legible on transactional screens" caveat).

### Motion & texture
Sparse and purposeful: a film-grain overlay on hero surfaces, live-status pulse
dots, and the animated queue-claim demo. Motion signals "live network" without
fighting the imagery.

## 8. Audience & positioning

### Persona A — the scaling company owner (primary buyer)
Runs a real-estate media company; growth is capped by shooter hours; loses sleep
over churn, training, and consistency. **Wants:** a reliable bench and a way to
cover overflow/vacations/quits without dropping quality. **Message:** _"Your next
shoot shouldn't depend on one person showing up."_

### Persona B — the freelance photographer (supply, the flywheel)
Skilled shooter who wants steady work without the hustle, fair rates, and a
reputation that travels. **Wants:** claimable jobs that fit their schedule, no
bidding wars, a portfolio that sells them. **Message:** _"Steady freelance work,
on your terms — and a rating that follows you everywhere."_

### Positioning statement
> For photography companies that can't scale past a single shooter's calendar,
> **Callsheet** is the bench-and-queue network that keeps every shoot covered by
> a trained, rated photographer — so growth never waits on hiring. Unlike
> on-demand marketplaces that optimize for the cheapest one-off booking,
> Callsheet optimizes for **consistency and reliability at volume**.

### Messaging pillars
1. **Reliability** — the shoot date always holds. (Counter to the documented
   scheduling-fragility failure.)
2. **Consistency** — a trained, style-matched bench, not a stranger each time.
3. **Scale without hiring** — post to a queue; the first qualified pro claims it.
4. **Transparency** — rates and equipment policy visible up front, for both
   sides.

### Taglines to test
- _"The bench that never misses a shoot."_ (recommended default)
- _"Build a bench. Claim the queue."_
- _"Consistency, on call."_

## 9. Go-to-market notes

- **Seed the supply-constrained side first.** The bench is the moat; recruit
  photographers around a few anchor companies (the real-estate vertical is the
  sharpest wedge — clear deliverables, repeatable workflow, documented churn).
- **Lead with reliability ROI to companies**, using the sell-faster / sell-for-
  more listing stats (traced to primary sources) — the buyer already believes in
  photography's ROI; sell them _uptime_.
- **Make portfolios the acquisition surface for photographers** — the micro-site
  is both the product and the referral unit ("share your Callsheet").
- **Avoid the Meero trap:** don't compete on being the cheapest booking. Compete
  on trained consistency and never-miss scheduling.
